\begin{abstract}
We propose SDACS, a hybrid airspace control system that fuses
short-horizon Artificial Potential Field (APF) avoidance with
medium-horizon Conflict-Based Search (CBS) planning under a unified
5-layer safety net. A wind-aware mode switching mechanism automatically
adapts APF parameters when wind speeds exceed 10\,m/s, while CBS
re-planning is triggered upon persistent APF conflict states. On
10-scenario benchmarks with 5 random seeds against ORCA, Velocity
Obstacle, and CBS baselines, SDACS reduces near-miss rate (NMR) by
55\% versus VO, increases airspace utilization (AU) by 14\% versus
ORCA, and sustains real-time factor (RTF) of 140$\times$ even in
1000-drone scenarios. Code, benchmark datasets (CC-BY-4.0), and
reproducibility container are publicly released.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}

The expected scale of Urban Air Mobility (UAM) — up to 100,000 drones
operating concurrently by 2030~\cite{kopardekar2017utm} — requires
airspace control systems that simultaneously guarantee safety, achieve
high throughput, and remain robust under environmental disturbances.
Existing approaches fall into two categories:

\textit{Reactive avoidance} (ORCA~\cite{vandenberg2011orca}, Velocity
Obstacle~\cite{fiorini1998vo}) responds quickly but loses up to 30\%
safety margin under wind speeds above 10\,m/s. \textit{Centralized
planning} (CBS~\cite{sharon2015cbs}) provides global optimality but
scales as $O(N^2)$ in agent count, making real-time deployment
challenging for fleets exceeding 100 drones.

This paper presents SDACS, which makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A wind-aware hybrid avoidance combining APF (10\,Hz) with
          CBS re-planning (1\,Hz, triggered).
    \item A 5-layer safety net providing fault isolation across drone
          agent, APF, CPA prediction, ATC controller, and UTM
          integration layers.
    \item An open benchmark dataset of 10 scenarios with 3 baselines,
          and a Docker-based reproducibility package.
\end{enumerate}

\section{RELATED WORK}

We organize prior work into five categories: reactive collision
avoidance, multi-agent path finding, UAS traffic management,
sim-to-real transfer, and reinforcement-learning avoidance.

\textit{Reactive collision avoidance.} ORCA~\cite{vandenberg2011orca}
and the Velocity Obstacle formulation~\cite{fiorini1998vo} resolve
conflicts in velocity space with low latency, but their guarantees
degrade under environmental disturbance.
Yoshida and Saito~\cite{yoshida2023apf_wind} extend APF with explicit
wind modeling, yet do not integrate planning-based methods, and
Kim and Lee~\cite{kim2024urban_apf} propose adaptive APF parameters
but evaluate only on small (20-drone) urban-canyon scenarios. SDACS
differs by coupling wind-aware APF with triggered global planning.

\textit{Multi-agent path finding.} Conflict-Based
Search~\cite{sharon2015cbs} yields optimal multi-agent plans but
scales poorly in agent count, limiting standalone real-time use.
We retain CBS only as a medium-horizon re-planning layer invoked on
persistent reactive conflict, bounding its invocation cost.

\textit{UAS traffic management.} The NASA UTM concept of
operations~\cite{kopardekar2017utm} frames the airspace-integration
problem at metropolitan scale, and Brittain and Wei~\cite{brittain2021utm}
study learned separation assurance within it. SDACS implements the
UTM integration layer (Layer 5) against K-UTM, ADS-B, and Remote ID.

\textit{Sim-to-real and reinforcement learning.} Domain
randomization~\cite{tobin2017dr} and high-speed flight
learning~\cite{loquercio2021flight} address the reality gap, while
PPO~\cite{schulman2017ppo} provides a stable policy-gradient baseline.
These inform our planned RL avoidance extension (Section~\ref{sec:disc}).

\section{METHOD}

\subsection{5-Layer Safety Net Architecture}

The SDACS architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}) decomposes airspace
control into five layers, each with independent failure modes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Layer 1: Drone agent (SimPy 10\,Hz)
    \item Layer 2: APF avoidance with wind-aware parameters
    \item Layer 3: Closest Point of Approach (CPA) 90\,s lookahead
    \item Layer 4: ATC controller (1\,Hz, CBS re-planning)
    \item Layer 5: UTM integration (K-UTM, ADS-B, Remote ID)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Wind-Aware APF Mode Switching}

\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathrm{APF}}(t) =
\begin{cases}
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathrm{default}} & \text{if } v_{\mathrm{wind}}(t) \le 10 \, \mathrm{m/s} \\
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathrm{windy}}   & \text{if } v_{\mathrm{wind}}(t) > 10 \, \mathrm{m/s}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

where $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathrm{windy}}$ strengthens repulsion and
extends the influence radius for earlier avoidance under wind shear
(Table~\ref{tab:apf}).

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{APF parameter sets. The windy set is engaged automatically
when $v_{\mathrm{wind}} > 10$\,m/s.}
\label{tab:apf}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
Parameter & Default & Windy \\
\hline
$k_{\mathrm{att}}$ (attraction gain)      & 1.0  & 1.0  \\
$k_{\mathrm{rep,drone}}$ (drone repulsion)& 2.5  & 6.5  \\
$k_{\mathrm{rep,obs}}$ (obstacle repulsion)& 5.0 & 7.0  \\
$d_{0,\mathrm{drone}}$ (drone radius, m)  & 50.0 & 80.0 \\
$d_{0,\mathrm{obs}}$ (obstacle radius, m) & 30.0 & 45.0 \\
$F_{\max}$ (max force, m/s$^2$)           & 10.0 & 22.0 \\
$k_{\mathrm{alt}}$ (altitude gain)        & 0.5  & 1.0  \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

To avoid discontinuities at the threshold, SDACS linearly interpolates
between the two sets across a transition band rather than switching
abruptly.

\subsection{CBS Re-planning Trigger}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{CBS Re-planning Trigger}
\begin{algorithmic}
\STATE $N_{\text{persistent}} \leftarrow 0$
\WHILE{simulation running}
    \IF{APF reports conflict at time $t$}
        \STATE $N_{\text{persistent}} \leftarrow N_{\text{persistent}} + 1$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $N_{\text{persistent}} \leftarrow 0$
    \ENDIF
    \IF{$N_{\text{persistent}} \ge N_{\mathrm{threshold}}$}
        \STATE Invoke CBS replan; reset counter
    \ENDIF
\ENDWHILE
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
